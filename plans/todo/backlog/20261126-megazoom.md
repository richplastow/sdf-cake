## Problem

The crumb is a fractal field, so in principle the view could zoom into a
sponge hole, then into the holes in its wall, without end. Today the
zoom stops at `MAX_ZOOM_RATIO`, 3, in `docs/src/main.js`; the crumb has
`HOLE_OCTAVES`, 2, fixed in `docs/src/sdf/crumb.js`; and
`SURFACE_EPSILON_METERS`, 0.1 mm, and `NORMAL_EPSILON_METERS`, 0.5 mm,
are fixed in `docs/src/raymarch/fragment.js`, with the camera's near
plane at 1 cm, so a zoom past a few centimetres would find no finer
detail and would break on the epsilons and the near plane.

## Current position

[Plan 14](../14-better-sponge.md) fixes the crumb at the scale a visitor
sees it. The zoom cap stays, since the field below it is not built.

## Recommended fix: octaves windowed to the pixel, epsilons to the ray

- Each hole octave halves the cell, so the octaves between the pixel's
  footprint and `CRUMB_CLIP_METERS` are the only ones that can show. Run
  the octave loop from the first octave whose cell is under the clip to
  the last whose cell is above the footprint, both found from the ray's
  distance to the camera: a near constant three or so octaves at any
  zoom, so the frame costs about what it does today. GLSL ES 3.00 allows
  a loop bound that is not a constant, unlike ES 1.00.
- Scale `SURFACE_EPSILON_METERS` and `NORMAL_EPSILON_METERS` with the
  ray's distance, the pixel-footprint stop the research cited from
  [Enhanced Sphere Tracing](../01-presentation-mode-research.md#sources),
  and the camera's near plane with the zoom.
- Lift the zoom cap while the pointer is over the cake and the zoom
  deepens.
- The limit is the 32-bit float: about seven significant digits, so a
  field at positions of 0.1 m resolves to about 10 nm, and a zoom of
  four orders, from the 10 cm cake to features of 10 µm, is in reach
  before the noise shows. Not infinite, but far past what a visitor
  will reach.
- The cost claims above are derived, not measured. A spike plan should
  measure steps per pixel and frame time at four and six octaves, in a
  port of the field as Plan 08's research did, before any build.

## Other options

- A separate microscope page with its own small field, cheaper to build
  but a second crumb to keep in step with the real one.
- More fixed octaves and a deeper zoom cap, the simplest change, which
  costs every pixel eight sphere evaluations per octave per step whether
  zoomed or not.

## When to pick it up

After Plan 14 is done, if the demo wants a showpiece: first a spike plan
with the measurements above, then a build plan.
