## 2. Intro (Pancake)

The word 'cake' comes from the Old Norse _kaka_.  
But nobody's really sure where that word comes from.

For the first few hundred years, a cake was just bread that you'd flipped over:
- Bread stayed the right way up.
- If you turned it over on the hearthstone, it was a cake.

So basically, pancakes were a typical ancient British breakfast.

## 3. Ancient Egypt (Pepi'Onkh Honey Cake)

The world's oldest **surviving** cake is Egyptian, and more than 4,000 years old.  
It was found in 1913 in the tomb of a vizier called Pepi'Onkh,  
and would fit nicely in the palm of your hand.  

It's two wheat flatbreads, filled with honey, with sesame seeds on top.  
It was baked between two tight-fitting copper moulds,  
and as it cooled, the escaping air formed a vacuum seal, which is why it survived four thousand years.

You can go and see it today. It's in a food museum in Switzerland.

## 4. Offerings to the gods (Artemis Offering)

The Ancient Greeks gave the world the important innovation of putting candles on cakes.  
But it wasn't for birthdays.  
Once a month they offered round cakes covered with candles to **Artemis,** the goddess of the moon.  

## 5. Cake wars

In 1832 in Vienna, Prince Metternich's head chef fell ill just before an important dinner, and a 16-year-old apprentice called Franz Sacher had to improvise a dessert from whatever he could find.

He invented The Sachertorte.

A century later, Hotel Sacher went bankrupt, and Sacher's son joined the rival confectioner Demel. There was a big legal battle over who could call it "The Original Sachertorte" that went on so long it's known as the **Sweet Seven Years' War.** The 1963 settlement: Hotel Sacher gets two layers of apricot jam and a round chocolate seal. Demel gets one layer of jam and a triangular seal.

_Hotel Sacher still makes 360,000 tortes a year, by hand. They say never put it in the fridge, and that it fits in cabin luggage._

## 6. Anthill Cake

- **Anthill Cake** (Brazil, _Bolo Formigueiro_): a sponge full of chocolate sprinkles, so every slice looks like it's swarming with ants.

## 7. Cow Cake

- **Cow Cake** (Vietnam, _Bánh Bò_): a pandan and coconut sponge, named because the honeycomb sponge looks like the lining of a cow's stomach.
